IAS Webinar on IoT and AI technology draws 83 participants from accross the country

By Administrator · Activities
20 September, 2026

The IEEE Industry Applications Society (IAS) Indonesia Chapter, in collaboration with the IEEE East Java Subsection and the IEEE Northern Sumatra Subsection, held the Webinar IoT dan Teknologi AI on Saturday, 18 July 2026, from 15:30 to 17:00 WIB. The session took place online via Zoom Meeting, and 83 participants completed the attendance and evaluation form.

Speakers from industry and academia presented applications of the Internet of Things (IoT) and artificial intelligence (AI) in industrial safety, agriculture, and heart disease diagnosis.

Highlights

Productivity, safety and security in industry with AI, IoT and compression

Mauritz Panggabean, PhD (AssistX), Chief Scientific Officer of AssistX, presented the products and solutions of this Indonesian research-based technology company, which was established in 2018. The talk focused on two areas. The first is computer vision for CCTV surveillance: an edge device compresses live video streams, reported as more than 90% more efficient with an average SSIM (structural similarity) of 0.987, while AI analytics such as people counting, intrusion detection and tamper detection run on the compressed streams. The second is intelligent document processing, which uses generative AI and vision AI to classify documents and extract data from ID cards, CVs and bank statements.

According to the presentation, one banking client monitoring more than 6,000 ATMs and branch offices reduced its response time to potential fraud incidents from one to two weeks to 10 to 15 minutes. A defence-sector client raised document processing from up to 250,000 to more than 6 million documents per year, about 24 times faster.

Agricultural mechanization with automatic tractor control

Dr. Purba Daru Kusuma (Universitas Telkom) described an IoT-based monitoring system for agricultural machinery, developed for the UPTD BP Mektan agricultural mechanization unit in West Java. He identified three problems: rental records kept by hand, no way to see where machines are, and no reliable record of whether a machine is working or idle.

The proposed design uses GPS and voltage and current sensors on the machine battery, an ESP32 microcontroller that formats the readings, a cellular module that sends them to a server, and a web dashboard showing asset location and status. The benefits presented were real-time monitoring, accurate operating-hour logs and better management of regional assets.

Artificial intelligence and heart health

Riandini, S.T., M.Sc. (Politeknik Negeri Jakarta) explained why AI suits cardiology: almost every heart examination produces digital data, including ECG, echocardiography, CT and MRI. She reviewed how deep learning is used in each modality and used myocardial scar segmentation on cardiac MRI as a case study, with U-Net based networks as the main architecture family.

She also raised the challenges of generalising models to data from other hospitals, and stressed that AI supports doctors rather than replaces them (human-in-the-loop). Trends she listed include foundation models, federated learning, explainable AI and digital twins. Clinical use still needs external validation, interpretability, safety and close collaboration between computer scientists, doctors and regulators.

Attendance and feedback

The audience was diverse. Most participants were university students and lecturers, ranging from undergraduate to doctoral level, joined by professionals from industry and other organisations. They came from universities, a polytechnic, a higher education service office and private companies in cities across Sumatra, Java, Sulawesi and Nusa Tenggara.

Their interests were just as varied. Participants named industrial automation, healthcare and biomedical engineering, power systems, telecommunications, renewable energy, education and robotics among the topics they wish to see next, which shows how widely IoT and AI now reach across fields.

Overall satisfaction averaged 4.71 out of 5: 62 participants gave 5, 18 gave 4 and 3 gave 3. Participants also rated four aspects of the webinar:

  • Speaker delivery: 79 of 83 rated it good or very good (45 very good).
  • Audio and visual quality: 76 rated it good or very good, and 7 rated it fair.
  • Depth of material: 76 rated it good or very good, 6 fair and 1 poor.
  • Relevance to their needs: 76 rated it good or very good, 6 fair and 1 poor.

When asked whether they would join a future webinar, 42 participants (51%) said they would definitely attend, 40 (48%) answered maybe, depending on the topic, and 1 said no.

Suggestions for future topics

Participants asked for more on AI in industrial automation and manufacturing, machine learning and deep learning, and AI in healthcare and biomedical engineering. Other topics mentioned were cyber security, renewable energy, natural language processing, robotics and AI in education. Two participants asked for a short live demonstration, and one suggested a later start time.
