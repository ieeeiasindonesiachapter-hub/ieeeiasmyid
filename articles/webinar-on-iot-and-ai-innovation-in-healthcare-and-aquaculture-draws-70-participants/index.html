Webinar on IoT and AI innovation in healthcare and aquaculture draws 70 participants

By Administrator · Activities
21 September, 2026

The IEEE Industry Applications Society (IAS) Indonesia Chapter, in collaboration with the IEEE East Java Subsection and the IEEE Northern Sumatra Subsection, held the webinar Inovasi IoT dan AI di Bidang Kesehatan dan Industri Budidaya Perikanan (Innovation of IoT and AI in Healthcare and the Aquaculture Industry) on 19 September 2026. The session ran from 09:00 to 11:00 WIB online through Zoom Meeting, and 70 participants attended.

Highlights

Prof. Ir. Khairul Anam (Universitas Jember)

The talk, Mind, Muscle, and Machine: AI-Powered Wearables untuk Gerak Manusia, began with the need for assistive technology. According to the presentation, Indonesia has 22.97 million people with disabilities, about 8.5% of the population, and a stroke prevalence of 8.3 per 1,000 people. About half of stroke survivors are said to live with long-term disability, while affordable consumer assistive devices remain scarce.

The approach rests on three research pillars of the Intelligent Systems and Robotics Lab at Universitas Jember. Bioelectric signals (EMG and EEG) are filtered, turned into features and passed to deep learning models such as CNN and LSTM, which drive the device. The first pillar is a smart prosthetic hand that recognises up to eight finger poses and predicts fingertip force in real time, with joint research reported with Friedrich-Alexander University Erlangen-Nuremberg in Germany. The second is a hand exoskeleton for stroke rehabilitation that combines game-based therapy with EMG or EEG control, and the slides mention a granted patent for a portable robot and gamification rehabilitation device.

The third pillar is a smart wheelchair that can be controlled by voice, EMG, EEG motor imagery or head motion. According to the presentation, it uses LiDAR and a Jetson Orin NX board running ROS2 for autonomous navigation, has a panic button, and reached an IoU of 0.87 and a Dice score of 0.93 in outdoor corridor segmentation tests. It was tested with cerebral palsy patients on a Careindo 503 electric wheelchair base. The speaker described all three prototypes as moving from laboratory to consumer-ready products and invited research, funding, field-test and student collaborations.

Dr. Purba Daru Kusuma, ST., MT. (Universitas Telkom)

The talk, Sistem Monitoring Kualitas Air pada Budidaya Kepiting Vertikal untuk Multi Kolam dan Multi User, presented an IoT system for crab farming in a multi-pond apartment system. The prototype was developed to monitor more than one pond and to serve more than one user, and a GSM module was added because WiFi coverage can be limited. The project aims to reach technology readiness level 6, an operational test in the field. According to the presentation, the work moved from a 2024 model under a catalyst grant with Universitas Bina Nusantara and Universitas Surabaya, to a 2025 single-pond prototype, to the 2026 multi-pond, multi-user prototype.

The hardware measures pH, temperature and salinity and sends the data over GSM to a web server at krabcare.com. The slides report a laboratory test on 12 September 2026 at the Rnest laboratory of Universitas Telkom with three testers from Politeknik Negeri Bandung, covering software function, usability and hardware accuracy. The pH sensor had an average error below 2% at pH 4.01 and 9.1, the temperature sensor below 7%, and the salinity sensor about 5% at 0.4 g per 100 mL. Readings were weaker at some points, for example pH 3, temperatures near 13 degrees Celsius, and very low or higher salinity solutions.

On the software side, the slides note that predictions for 15 to 30 minutes ahead had very small error, while 60-minute predictions had large error, and the overall usability score was 4.7. The next steps named in the presentation are to finalise the prototype from the laboratory results, run a field test at aquaculture ponds in Surabaya, and revise the outputs.

Attendance and feedback

The participants were mostly university students and lecturers, with a smaller group from the general public. They came from universities and a few other organisations in many parts of Indonesia, from Sumatra and Java to Madura and Maluku, and most were not yet IEEE members. The main reasons for joining were to learn the basics of IoT, to understand how AI is used in industry, to find references for study and research, to follow current technology trends and to learn more about the Chapter and the two Subsections.
