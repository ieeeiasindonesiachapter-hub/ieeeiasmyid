Webinar on IoT and AI for community welfare presents fleet management, rural energy and IoT training

By Administrator · Activities
21 September, 2026

The IEEE Industry Applications Society (IAS) Indonesia Chapter, in collaboration with the IEEE East Java Subsection and the IEEE Northern Sumatra Subsection, held the Webinar Pemanfaatan IoT dan Kecerdasan Buatan bagi Kesejahteraan Masyarakat (the use of IoT and artificial intelligence for community welfare) on Saturday, 22 August 2026, from 15:30 to 17:00 WIB. The session took place online via Zoom Meeting, and 50 participants attended.

Three speakers from industry and academia showed how the Internet of Things (IoT) and related technologies can serve logistics, rural communities and education.

Highlights

OSLOG: digital fleet management and logistics

Handy Madinata, S.TI. (Founder and CEO, PT. iSense Technology; PT Integrasia Utama) presented OSLOG Datatec, a platform for fleet management and logistics. He described common problems: fleets that cannot be tracked in real time, rising fuel and maintenance costs, driver behaviour that is hard to measure, and proof of delivery still done by hand.

According to the presentation, the platform combines telematics, cloud computing, data analytics and safety sensors around three elements: vehicle, driver and operation. It records braking, stopping, lane changes, turning at intersections and overall ride comfort, and turns these data into a driver score, a driver ranking and a risk analysis that support safety coaching. Other features include route planning, electronic proof of delivery, driver fatigue monitoring and a digital audit trail of fleet activity.

Smart power electronics for rural and coastal energy independence

Prof. Aripriharta, S.T., M.T., PhD. (Universitas Negeri Malang) argued that electricity is now almost everywhere in Indonesia but still comes mostly from fossil fuel. The slides cite a 99.83% electrification ratio, a fossil share of 83.7% in power generation in 2025, and 12,968 coastal villages. Rural and coastal areas have strong solar, wind and water resources, but fluctuating supply makes conventional systems fragile.

His answer is smart power electronics: converters with intelligent control that keep voltage stable and extract maximum power. The slides present a swarm-intelligence maximum power point tracking method and report efficiency of 95.0 to 99.1% against about 34% for a conventional algorithm under shading. He proposed micro-grids of 500 W to 1 kW built on three pillars: solar panels, a small vertical-axis wind turbine for coastal sites, and pico-hydro with an Archimedes screw for irrigation channels. According to the slides, levelised cost is about Rp 740 per kWh for solar and about Rp 489 per kWh for pico-hydro in the simulation, against a non-subsidised grid tariff of Rp 1,444.70 to Rp 1,501 per kWh, with payback in two to four years. Field examples from East Java included street lights, a 5 kWp system at a boarding school, wind turbines for a coffee stall and pico-hydro units.

SEMOTE-G: an IoT trainer for education

Dr. Ir. Made Santo Gitakarma, S.T., M.T., MCE. (Universitas Pendidikan Ganesha) introduced the Smart Environmental Monitoring Trainer for Education - Ganesha (SEMOTE-G). He began with the basics of IoT, its six building blocks and common communication protocols.

The trainer uses a NodeMCU ESP8266 microcontroller with temperature and humidity, air pressure, light and distance sensors. It sends readings as JSON messages over MQTT, a lightweight publish and subscribe protocol, to a Mosquitto broker on a Raspberry Pi 4. Node-RED on the same Raspberry Pi then processes the data and shows it on a web dashboard with gauges and charts. The whole system runs on a local network, which, according to the presentation, keeps data private and independent of the public internet.

Attendance and feedback

The audience was diverse. Most participants were university students and lecturers, from undergraduate to doctoral level, joined by researchers, industry professionals and small business owners. They connected from universities and institutions across Java, Sumatra, Bali, Nusa Tenggara and Kalimantan. Their main reasons for joining were to learn IoT basics, to understand AI in industry and to follow technology trends.
